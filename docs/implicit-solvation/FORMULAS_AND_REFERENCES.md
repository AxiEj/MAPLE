# Route-2 formula and reference ledger

## Route 2: self-consistent polarizable MLIP--PCM/SMD coupling

Route 2 is the **Research/Innovation Route**. Its current implementation
provides an energy proof-of-concept for one fixed conformer:

\[
\Delta G_{\mathrm{solv}}
=\Delta E_{\mathrm{solute}}+U_{\mathrm{pol}}+G_{\mathrm{CDS}},
\]

\[
E_{\mathrm{reported}}
=E_{\mathrm{MACE,gas}}+\Delta G_{\mathrm{solv}}.
\]

It is not route 3: the official MACE-POLAR-1-M checkpoint was trained as a
general polarizable MLIP, not fine-tuned against implicit-solvent free
energies. MAPLE trains nothing and changes no checkpoint weight.

This fixed-conformer result is not yet a complete solution-phase PES.
FreeSolv is useful as an energy diagnostic, but Route 2 is defined by
self-consistent mutual polarization and an energy-consistent derivative, not
by hydration-MAE tuning.

### Coarse-grained density and molecular electrostatic potential

MACE-POLAR returns four coefficients per atom: one normalized monopole and
three real-spherical \(l=1\) components. MAPLE applies the upstream e3nn
permutation to obtain an atom-centred charge \(q_i\) and Cartesian dipole
\(\mathbf p_i\). These coefficients describe a coarse-grained residual
**net-charge density**, not the all-electron or valence-electron density of a
QM wavefunction.

MACE-POLAR-1 internally uses a Gaussian width
\(\sigma=1.5\,\mathring{\mathrm A}\) for its learned long-range
electrostatics. That smearing is comparable to the atomic SMD cavity radii and
is therefore not extended across the dielectric boundary: doing so suppresses
the near-field cavity MEP even when the molecular dipole is correct.

\[
V_{\mathrm{solute}}(\mathbf s)
=\sum_i\left[
\frac{q_i}{|\mathbf s-\mathbf R_i|}
+\frac{\mathbf p_i\cdot(\mathbf s-\mathbf R_i)}
{|\mathbf s-\mathbf R_i|^3}
\right],
\]

MAPLE instead evaluates the cavity-exterior \(l\le 1\) point-multipole
expansion at each PCMSolver tessera centre. In atomic units this gives the
surface vector \(\mathbf V_{\mathrm{solute}}\). The learned coefficients and
the MACE model itself are unchanged.

PCMSolver solves aqueous IEFPCM with explicit SMD Coulomb radii and returns
integrated apparent surface charges \(\boldsymbol\sigma_{\mathrm{ASC}}\).
Its public polarization-energy routine already evaluates

\[
U_{\mathrm{pol}}
=\frac12\,
\mathbf V_{\mathrm{solute}}^{T}\boldsymbol\sigma_{\mathrm{ASC}}.
\]

MAPLE checks that convention at runtime and does not apply another factor of
one half. The same exterior point-multipole kernel projects the ASC back to the
atom-centred reaction potential and gradient. The reciprocal identity

\[
\sum_i
\left(q_i V_i+\mathbf p_i\cdot\nabla V_i\right)
=\mathbf V_{\mathrm{solute}}^{T}\boldsymbol\sigma_{\mathrm{ASC}}
\]

must hold to floating-point tolerance or the calculation fails.

The cavity is generated by PCMSolver in `MODE=ATOMS` with scaling disabled and
the SMD Coulomb radii. The primary tessera target area is
\(0.2\,\mathring{\mathrm A}^2\). If PCMSolver reports a matrix warning, MAPLE
retries with \(0.28\,\mathring{\mathrm A}^2\) and a
\(0.30\,\mathring{\mathrm A}\) minimum added-sphere radius; a warning from the
fallback fails closed.

| H | C | N | O | F | P | S | Cl | Br | I |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.20 | 1.85 | 1.89 | 1.52 | 1.73 | 2.47 | 2.12 | 2.49 | 2.60 | 2.74 |

The Br and I values are the revised Minnesota values.

### Polarizable response

The upstream MACE-POLAR model already contains a GTO
potential/field-to-feature projector. MAPLE supplies the local
\([V_i,\nabla V_i]\) values through that projector, leaving the graph-level
uniform external field at zero. Consequently the returned quantity is the
field-polarized **intrinsic** MACE energy and deliberately excludes the
explicit \(\langle\rho,V_{\mathrm{reac}}\rangle\) interaction:

\[
\Delta E_{\mathrm{solute}}
=E_{\mathrm{MACE,intrinsic}}[V_{\mathrm{reac}}]
 -E_{\mathrm{MACE,gas}}.
\]

This convention is why MAPLE does not subtract the density/reaction coupling a
second time. It is locked by implementation and audit provenance; a future
adapter whose energy includes the explicit coupling would require a different
formula and profile name.

For `response=scf`, MAPLE iterates

\[
\rho^{(n)}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{IEFPCM}} V_{\mathrm{reac}}^{(n)}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{MACE-POLAR}} \rho_{\mathrm{response}}^{(n)}
\]

with 0.5 density mixing, a maximum of 50 iterations, density tolerance
\(10^{-5}\,e\), and intrinsic-energy tolerance \(10^{-5}\,\mathrm{eV}\).
Non-convergence fails closed. `response=frozen` performs only the first PCM
solve and sets \(\Delta E_{\mathrm{solute}}=0\); it is a diagnostic, not the
public default. Float64 is required because this response energy is a small
difference between large absolute MLIP energies.

### Native aqueous SMD CDS term

MAPLE evaluates the SMD cavity/dispersion/solvent-structure contribution as

\[
G_{\mathrm{CDS}}\,[\mathrm{kcal/mol}]
=\frac{1}{1000}\sum_i \gamma_i A_i,
\]

where the aqueous atomic surface tension \(\gamma_i\) includes the published
geometry-dependent switching terms and \(A_i\) is the solvent-accessible area
from the SMD/Bondi radius plus the \(0.4\,\mathring{\mathrm A}\) SMD probe.
Areas use a deterministic 5810-point-per-atom equal-area spherical quadrature.
Static water, methane, and methanol values agree with NWChem `mnsol.F` within
`0.015 kcal/mol`; NWChem and PySCF are not runtime dependencies.

The standard state is exactly 1 M gas to 1 M solution:

\[
\Delta G_{\mathrm{standard\ state}}=0.
\]

MAPLE therefore does not add the approximately `1.89 kcal/mol` 1 atm to 1 M
correction.

### Force derivative: next primary milestone

For a converged density representation \(c^*(\mathbf R)\) and apparent surface
charge \(\sigma^*(\mathbf R)\), the required solvent force is the total
derivative

\[
\mathbf F_A^{\mathrm{solv}}
=-\frac{d}{d\mathbf R_A}
\left[
\Delta E_{\mathrm{solute}}(\mathbf R,c^*)
+\frac12\mathbf V(\mathbf R,c^*)^T\sigma^*
+G_{\mathrm{CDS}}(\mathbf R)
\right].
\]

The current fixed-point SCF implementation has not yet established a
variational functional with respect to both learned density coefficients and
ASC. Therefore response terms such as \(dc^*/d\mathbf R_A\) and
\(d\sigma^*/d\mathbf R_A\) must not be discarded merely by analogy to a
stationary QM-SCF wavefunction.

An energy-consistent implementation must account for all of the following:

1. the gas-to-polarized intrinsic MACE-POLAR force difference;
2. derivatives of the solute MEP and its local reaction potential/gradient;
3. derivatives of the PCM boundary operators, tessera centres/areas, and ASC;
4. the converged mutual-response derivative, either by a proven stationary
   functional or an explicit coupled-response/implicit-differentiation solve;
5. the analytic derivative of the geometry-dependent SMD CDS/SASA term.

The installed PCMSolver v1.1.12-style C interface used by MAPLE exposes cavity
geometry, ASC, and polarization energy, but no nuclear-gradient API. Numerical
whole-energy finite differences are therefore a reference oracle for
derivation and testing, not an acceptable production-force substitute.
Production force support requires an analytic/differentiable PCM boundary and
CDS path. The current hard-visibility Shrake--Rupley point count is not
differentiable, and warning-triggered switching between two cavity definitions
is not a valid PES rule. These must be resolved before translational/rotational
invariance, central-difference agreement, optimization, or MD.
