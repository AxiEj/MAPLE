# Terminal decision

**The neutral-atom Gaussian penetration source is admissible in a stationary, parameter-free continuum electrostatic theory, but not as a boundary-only add-on to unchanged standard ddPCM/CPCM.**

It is admissible only when the analytic Gaussian mixture is treated as an actual three-dimensional charge density and the continuum model includes its exterior-volume polarization. For a sharp cavity, that means the exact Poisson/SVPE formulation—or an independently verified equivalent—not merely adding (\delta V|_\Gamma) to the molecular electrostatic potential and retaining the standard (\tfrac12 v^\top q) ledger.

This conclusion applies to the **continuum subproblem for a prescribed source**. It does not turn the complete MACE-POLAR fixed-point construction into a common variational MACE+PCM functional.

A second, independent conclusion is terminal: **the frozen outputs do not uniquely determine any local (l\ge 2) source completion.** Free-atom translations determine the dipolar ADT tangent already used, not a unique quadrupole or octupole model. Therefore, if the current candidate fails a frozen source/chemical gate and an exact escaped-charge test does not close that failure, the zero-training near-field route is exhausted.

# I. Structural proof

## 1. Exact stationary coupling of an outlying Gaussian source

The potential in the prompt corresponds to the signed density

[
\delta\rho_A(\mathbf r)
=======================

## Z_A\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_A)

\sum_k N_{Ak}
\left(\frac{\alpha_{Ak}}{\pi}\right)^{3/2}
e^{-\alpha_{Ak}|\mathbf r-\mathbf R_A|^2},
\qquad
\sum_k N_{Ak}=Z_A .
]

Thus

[
C,\delta\rho_A
==============

\delta V_A,
]

where (C=(-\nabla^2/4\pi)^{-1}) is the vacuum Coulomb operator. The source is neutral and has zero dipole, but every untruncated Gaussian has nonzero support outside every finite cavity.

Let (\Omega_R) be the solute cavity and let

[
\epsilon_R(\mathbf r)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\mathbf r\in\Omega_R,\
\epsilon_s,&\mathbf r\notin\Omega_R.
\end{cases}
]

For a prescribed total source

[
\rho=\rho_{\mathrm{POLAR},q/p}+\rho_{\mathrm{ADT}}+\delta\rho_{\mathrm{pen}},
]

the correct parent field functional, in Gaussian units, is

[
\mathcal J_R[\phi;\rho]
=======================

\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb R^3}
\epsilon_R(\mathbf r)|\nabla\phi(\mathbf r)|^2,d\mathbf r
---------------------------------------------------------

\int_{\mathbb R^3}\rho(\mathbf r)\phi(\mathbf r),d\mathbf r.
]

Stationarity gives

[
-\nabla\cdot\left(\epsilon_R\nabla\phi_\epsilon\right)=4\pi\rho,
]

while the vacuum reference satisfies

[
-\nabla^2\phi_0=4\pi\rho.
]

The polarization energy is therefore

[
G_{\mathrm{pol}}[\rho,R]
========================

\frac12
\int\rho(\mathbf r)
\left[\phi_\epsilon(\mathbf r)-\phi_0(\mathbf r)\right]d\mathbf r
=================================================================

\frac12\langle\rho,W_R\rho\rangle ,
]

where (W_R) is the self-adjoint reaction-field operator. This Poisson equation and the (\tfrac12\int\rho\phi_{\rm rxn}) energy are the canonical continuum starting point. ([arXiv][1])

Consequently, if the penetration source is added to a base source (\rho_0),

[
\boxed{
\Delta G_{\mathrm{pen}}
=======================

\langle\delta\rho_{\mathrm{pen}},W_R\rho_0\rangle
+
\frac12
\langle\delta\rho_{\mathrm{pen}},
W_R\delta\rho_{\mathrm{pen}}\rangle
}
]

with no empirical coefficient. This is the only physically natural parameter-free quadratic completion once (\delta\rho_{\mathrm{pen}}), (\epsilon_R), and the cavity are fixed.

### Sharp-cavity surface-plus-volume representation

For (\epsilon_{\rm in}=1), define the escaped-charge volume polarization

[
\beta_R(\mathbf r)
==================

\left(\epsilon_s^{-1}-1\right)
\chi_{\Omega_R^c}(\mathbf r)\rho(\mathbf r).
]

Let

[
\phi^\rho=C\rho,\qquad
\phi^\beta=C\beta_R.
]

The exact reaction potential is not merely the potential of a surface charge. It is

[
\boxed{
\phi_{\rm rxn}=S\sigma+\phi^\beta ,
}
]

where (S) is the single-layer surface-potential operator. With

[
f_\epsilon=\frac{\epsilon_s-1}{\epsilon_s+1},
]

the exact surface equation can be written

[
\boxed{
\left[
I-\frac{f_\epsilon}{2\pi}D^\dagger
\right]\sigma
=============

\frac{f_\epsilon}{2\pi}
\partial_n\left(\phi^\rho+\phi^\beta\right)\Big|_\Gamma .
}
]

Equivalently, its right-hand side contains both the normal field of the original source and the normal field generated by the exterior volume polarization. This is the exact SVPE construction for escaped charge; IEFPCM’s usual source-inside reduction is exact only when escaped charge vanishes. ([arXiv][1])

Coulomb reciprocity then gives the exact energy ledger

[
\begin{aligned}
G_{\mathrm{pol}}
&=
\frac12\int\rho\left(S\sigma+C\beta_R\right)d\mathbf r[2mm]
&=
\boxed{
\frac12\int_\Gamma
\phi^\rho(\mathbf s)\sigma(\mathbf s),dS
+
\frac12\int_{\Omega_R^c}
\phi^\rho(\mathbf r)\beta_R(\mathbf r),d\mathbf r .
}
\end{aligned}
]

The second term is the **direct volume-polarization contribution**. It is not optional. Chipman’s outlying-charge formulation was developed specifically because charge penetration creates both a modified surface state and a direct volume contribution to the energy. ([AIP Publishing][2])

### Required source map and receiver

For the exact sharp-cavity problem, the source map is not

[
\rho\longmapsto \gamma C\rho
]

alone. At minimum it is

[
\boxed{
T_R\rho
=======

\left(
\gamma C\rho,;
\gamma_n C(\rho+\beta_R),;
\beta_R
\right),
}
]

or an analytically equivalent potential-only reformulation that retains all information carried by (\beta_R).

Here:

* (\gamma) is the boundary trace;
* (\gamma_n) is the normal derivative;
* (\beta_R) is the exterior volume source.

The receiver conjugate to (\rho) is

[
\boxed{
\frac{\delta G_{\mathrm{pol}}}{\delta\rho}
==========================================

# \phi_{\rm rxn}

S\sigma+C\beta_R
}
]

evaluated on the **entire support of (\rho)**. It is not merely the surface potential or the potential evaluated at atom centers.

Therefore, supplying only (\delta V_A|_\Gamma) provides one piece of the exact source map, but omits both (\beta_R) and its contribution to the surface equation and energy.

### Nuclear-coordinate derivative ownership

At the continuum level,

[
G(R)=\frac12\langle\rho_R,W_R\rho_R\rangle
]

implies

[
\boxed{
\frac{dG}{dR_a}
===============

\left\langle
\frac{\partial\rho_R}{\partial R_a},
W_R\rho_R
\right\rangle
+
\frac12
\left\langle
\rho_R,
\frac{\partial W_R}{\partial R_a}\rho_R
\right\rangle .
}
]

For a smooth prescribed dielectric this becomes

[
\boxed{
\frac{dG}{dR_a}
===============

\int
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial R_a}
(\phi_\epsilon-\phi_0),d\mathbf r
---------------------------------

\frac{1}{8\pi}
\int
\frac{\partial\epsilon_R}{\partial R_a}
|\nabla\phi_\epsilon|^2,d\mathbf r .
}
]

The ownership is consequently:

1. **Source model**
   owns (\partial_{R_a}\rho|_u), including translation of the nuclear delta, Gaussian clouds, point multipoles, geometry dependence of POLAR outputs, and the ADT density.

2. **Sharp-cavity/outlying-charge module**
   owns the full derivative

   [
   \partial_{R_a}\beta_R
   =====================

   (\epsilon_s^{-1}-1)
   \left[
   \chi_{\Omega^c}\partial_{R_a}\rho
   +
   (\partial_{R_a}\chi_{\Omega^c})\rho
   \right].
   ]

   The second term is the moving-boundary contribution. It cannot be omitted merely because the Gaussian coefficients themselves are fixed.

3. **Continuum geometry**
   owns the cavity shape derivative, surface bases, exposure functions, quadrature weights, normal vectors, and derivatives of the ddPCM operators.

4. **Outer operational fixed point**
   owns the implicit (du^*/dR) contribution through the already declared JVP/VJP or adjoint machinery. None of this requires claiming a common MACE+PCM variational functional.

At the discrete level, an extension should be derived from a state-adjoint Lagrangian such as

[
\mathscr L(\sigma,\lambda;R)
============================

E_{\rm rec}(\rho,\sigma,\beta_R)
+
\lambda^\top
\left[A_R\sigma-b_R(\rho,\beta_R)\right],
]

with

[
A_R\sigma=b_R,\qquad
A_R^\top\lambda
===============

-\partial_\sigma E_{\rm rec}.
]

This matters because self-adjointness of the continuum reaction operator need not be preserved automatically by a collocation discretization; C-PCM/COSMO are special in this regard, whereas general IEF-type matrix representations can lose discrete self-adjointness. ([arXiv][1]) Existing ddPCM forces were themselves obtained from a complete derivative of the declared ddPCM energy, so an escaped-charge extension requires a correspondingly complete new derivative—not a boundary-potential patch. ([AIP Publishing][3])

## No-go theorem for unchanged boundary-only ddPCM

The exact coupling is impossible under the following simultaneous assumptions:

1. The declared physical model is the sharp-interface Poisson dielectric functional above.
2. Sources may be nonzero in (\Omega_R^c).
3. The continuum state is driven solely by boundary data derived from (C\rho).
4. No exterior volume source (\beta_R) is represented.
5. No direct volume term appears in the energy.
6. The construction is claimed exact for the allowed source class.

A strong counterexample is immediate. Choose a nonzero

[
w\in C_c^\infty(\Omega_R^c)
]

whose support is separated from the cavity boundary, and define

[
\rho_w=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\nabla^2w.
]

Its vacuum potential is (C\rho_w=w). Since (w) vanishes in a neighborhood of (\Gamma), its value, normal derivative, and every higher boundary derivative vanish. Any boundary-only solver therefore receives exactly the same data as for zero source.

Nevertheless, the exact dielectric solution is (w/\epsilon_s) on its support, and hence

[
\phi_{\rm rxn}
==============

\left(\epsilon_s^{-1}-1\right)w.
]

The exact polarization energy is

[
\begin{aligned}
G_w
&=
\frac12
\left(\epsilon_s^{-1}-1\right)
\int\rho_w w,d\mathbf r\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{1}{8\pi}
\left(\epsilon_s^{-1}-1\right)
\int|\nabla w|^2,d\mathbf r
}
<0 .
\end{aligned}
]

Thus even complete local boundary jets cannot determine the exact escaped-charge energy. A fortiori, (\delta V|_\Gamma) alone cannot.

For the specific Gaussian family, the analytic source parameters are known, so (\beta_R) can in principle be integrated explicitly. But that observation supports the **volume-polarization extension**; it does not rescue the boundary-only ledger.

## 2. The three density/cavity cases

### (a) Compact neutral spherical density entirely inside the solute region

Suppose the neutral spherical source has compact support inside its parent sphere and therefore inside (\Omega_R).

Outside its support, Newton’s theorem gives

[
C\delta\rho_A=0,
]

because the source is spherical and has zero total charge. Consequently:

[
\gamma C\delta\rho_A=0,\qquad
\beta_A=0,\qquad
\sigma[\delta\rho_A]=0.
]

There is also no cross polarization energy with another interior source. The reaction potential generated by the other source is harmonic throughout the support of (\delta\rho_A), and the spherical mean-value theorem gives

[
\int\delta\rho_A(\mathbf r)h(\mathbf r),d\mathbf r=0
]

for every such harmonic (h).

Therefore,

[
\boxed{
\Delta G_{\rm pen}=0
}
]

exactly. It cannot materially change the polarization energy.

This is not merely “small”; it is a structural zero. However, untruncated Gaussian mixtures never satisfy this case exactly for a finite cavity.

### (b) Analytic Gaussian tails cross the sharp boundary

For the actual Gaussian primitive,

[
\chi_{\Omega^c}\delta\rho_A\ne0
]

for every finite cavity. Then

[
\beta_A
=======

(\epsilon_s^{-1}-1)
\chi_{\Omega^c}\delta\rho_A
\ne0 .
]

The correction can now alter the surface state and polarization energy through:

[
\Delta\sigma,
\qquad
\frac12\langle\phi^\rho,\Delta\sigma\rangle_\Gamma,
\qquad
\frac12\langle\phi^\rho,\beta_A\rangle_{\Omega^c}.
]

It can therefore be material, but materiality is a numerical and chemical question, not a theorem. It is legitimate only under an exact or explicitly approximate escaped-charge functional with the corresponding volume receiver and derivatives.

Adding only (\delta V|_\Gamma) to standard ddPCM treats a non-harmonic exterior potential as though it were generated entirely by an interior source. That changes the operator semantics and omits the direct volume term.

### (c) Smooth isodensity or weighted dielectric

Let (\epsilon_R(\mathbf r)) vary smoothly. Then there is no special “inside-support” requirement: the parent Poisson functional remains valid for density everywhere.

The equivalent polarization density is

[
\rho_{\rm pol}
==============

\left(\frac1\epsilon-1\right)\rho
+
\frac{1}{4\pi}
\nabla\ln\epsilon\cdot\nabla\phi .
]

Thus Gaussian penetration is naturally incorporated as a volume effect.

Two subcases must be distinguished:

* If (\epsilon_R(\mathbf r)) is a fixed, geometry-defined field, the model is stationary and source-conjugate under the field functional already given.
* If (\epsilon) depends on the model density, then the dielectric changes when the penetration density changes. For a local (\epsilon(\rho)), the source derivative contains the additional term

  [
  \frac{\delta G}{\delta\rho}
  ===========================

  ## \phi_\epsilon-\phi_0

  \frac{1}{8\pi}
  \frac{\partial\epsilon}{\partial\rho}
  |\nabla\phi_\epsilon|^2 .
  ]

  Omitting this term destroys conjugacy.

Density-based smooth-continuum methods do provide such a Poisson-functional framework, but their dielectric switching definitions and interface parameters belong to a new cavity/electrostatic model, not to unchanged sharp-cavity ddPCM. ([AIP Publishing][4])

The case decision is therefore:

| Case                                          | Can penetration change (G_{\rm pol})? | Can it remain functional-consistent?                                             |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Compact neutral spherical source fully inside |                        No; exact zero | Yes, trivially                                                                   |
| Gaussian tails cross sharp boundary           |                                   Yes | Yes, only with escaped-charge volume polarization and full ledger                |
| Smooth/weighted dielectric                    |                                   Yes | Yes, for that newly declared Poisson functional, with all dielectric derivatives |

## 3. No uniquely determined zero-training local (l\ge2) completion

Let

[
Q_A\in V_2,\qquad O_A\in V_3,
]

where (V_l) is the (2l+1)-dimensional irreducible SO(3) representation. Per atom, this adds

[
\dim V_2+\dim V_3=5+7=12
]

unobserved components.

The frozen observables constrain:

* atomwise (q_A,p_A) from POLAR;
* changes in those (q/p) values under the native field;
* the molecular dipole;
* the molecular polarizability;
* geometry, elements, and spherical free-atom densities.

None of these observables changes when arbitrary intrinsic local quadrupoles and octupoles are added while (q_A,p_A) are held fixed.

### Explicit infinite family of indistinguishable completions

For any set of radial functions (f_{ZZ'}(r)) and (g_{ZZ'}(r)), define

[
Q_A^{(f)}
=========

\sum_{B\ne A}
f_{Z_AZ_B}(r_{AB})
,\mathrm{STF}
\left(
\mathbf r_{AB}\otimes\mathbf r_{AB}
\right),
]

and

[
O_A^{(g)}
=========

\sum_{B\ne A}
g_{Z_AZ_B}(r_{AB})
,\mathrm{STF}
\left(
\mathbf r_{AB}^{\otimes3}
\right).
]

These constructions are:

* translation invariant;
* permutation equivariant if the neighbor sums are used;
* SO(3)-equivariant;
* local if the radial functions have finite local support;
* compatible with exactly the same (q_A,p_A), molecular dipole, and uniform-field molecular polarizability.

The zero choice (f=g=0) and infinitely many nonzero choices are observationally indistinguishable under all frozen outputs. Therefore the observation map has at least the kernel

[
\boxed{
V_2^N\oplus V_3^N
}
]

for static multipoles, and an infinite-dimensional kernel when field-dependent (Q_A(u),O_A(u)) are allowed.

Geometry supplies possible tensor covariants; it does not determine their scalar amplitudes.

### Why (\alpha_{\rm MDP}) does not determine a quadrupole

The anisotropic part of a symmetric molecular polarizability transforms as (l=2), but representation type alone does not identify physical meaning.

A polarizability is

[
\alpha_{ij}
===========

\frac{\partial\mu_i}{\partial E_j},
]

whereas a quadrupole is conjugate to an external potential Hessian,

[
Q_{ij}
\sim
\frac{\partial E}{\partial(\partial_i\partial_j\Phi)}.
]

They have different units, different conjugate variables, different translation properties, and different response semantics. Even if one invented a dimensional conversion from a free-atom radial moment, there would be no unique conversion or atomic partition.

Moreover, six molecular polarizability components cannot determine (5N) local quadrupole components, much less (7N) local octupoles and their nonuniform response.

### Conjugacy obstruction

A physical local multipole expansion requires independent external-field jets:

[
\Phi_A,\quad
\nabla\Phi_A,\quad
\mathrm{STF},\nabla\nabla\Phi_A,\quad
\mathrm{STF},\nabla^3\Phi_A,\ldots
]

with (q,p,Q,O) obtained as derivatives of a scalar with respect to those conjugate coordinates.

The native POLAR input provides two-width (l\le1) potential features. Spatial variation of those features may contain information correlated with field curvature, but there is no canonical invertible map from those features to a local (l=2) or (l=3) potential jet, and there is no supervised scalar derivative identifying (Q_A) or (O_A). Constructing finite differences or geometry-dependent decoders would select one member of the infinite family above.

Unreleased backbone tensors do not solve this. Without supervision as electrostatic multipoles, a declared conjugate field coordinate, and an identifiable gauge, they remain latent representation variables.

Hence:

[
\boxed{
\text{No unique, SO(3)-equivariant, zero-training local }l\ge2
\text{ completion exists from the frozen data.}
}
]

The recent multipole-hierarchy preprint is consistent with the importance of (q/p/Q) hierarchies, but its environment-dependent multipoles are learned quantities; it supplies no zero-training identification theorem for the present checkpoints. ([arXiv][5])

## 4. Free-atom translations determine only the ADT dipolar tangent

For a spherical electronic density (n_A(r)), translated relative to a fixed positive core,

[
n_A(\mathbf r-\mathbf d)
========================

## n_A

d_i\partial_i n_A
+
\frac12d_id_j\partial_i\partial_jn_A
------------------------------------

\frac16d_id_jd_k\partial_i\partial_j\partial_kn_A
+\cdots .
]

The SO(3) content is

[
\partial_i n_A\in V_1,
]

[
\partial_i\partial_jn_A\in V_0\oplus V_2,
]

[
\partial_i\partial_j\partial_kn_A
\in V_1\oplus V_3.
]

### Linear response rank

The first-order density response is

[
\delta n_A^{(1)}
================

-\mathbf d_A\cdot\nabla n_A,
]

which is pure (l=1). Under a uniform field, the molecular input has three coordinates, so the canonical lift has the form

[
\mathbb R^3
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_A V_1 .
]

Its global rank is at most three:

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{ADT}_{\rm uniform})\le3.
}
]

This is precisely the dipolar ADT subspace already used.

The (V_2) term appears only at second order in displacement,

[
\delta n_A^{(2)}
\propto
d_{Ai}d_{Aj}\partial_i\partial_jn_A,
]

and the (V_3) term only at third order. Since (\mathbf d_A=O(E)),

[
Q_A^{\rm translation}=O(E^2),\qquad
O_A^{\rm translation}=O(E^3).
]

The molecular linear polarizability (\alpha_{\rm MDP}) fixes only the first-order total dipole. It does not fix these nonlinear coefficients, molecular hyperpolarizabilities, or responses to field gradients.

### Finite translation is one decoder, not a unique inference

A rigidly translated spherical cloud generates the special hierarchy

[
p_A\propto-N_A d_A,
]

[
Q_A\propto-N_A,\mathrm{STF}
(\mathbf d_A^{\otimes2}),
]

[
O_A\propto-N_A,\mathrm{STF}
(\mathbf d_A^{\otimes3}).
]

That hierarchy is mathematically legitimate **if finite rigid translation is declared as the physical density model**. It is not inferred uniquely from (p_A) or (\alpha_{\rm MDP}). Infinitely many deformations have the same first tangent and dipole but different second- and third-order tensors.

Using a permanent atomic dipole to set

[
\mathbf d_A=-\frac{\mathbf p_A}{N_A}
]

and then manufacturing (Q_A\propto d_A^2) and (O_A\propto d_A^3) would therefore be a chosen nonlinear decoder. It would not be a uniquely determined zero-training consequence of the checkpoint.

Finally, distributed atomwise (q/p) at different centers already generate higher **molecular far-field moments through translation of origins**. That is already included in their exact point-source potential. It is not the missing intrinsic local (l\ge2) near-field density around each atom.

# II. Numerical verification

## 5. Smallest target-independent terminal experiment

One case cannot separate all four mechanisms:

* a spherical atomic source has no local (l\ge2);
* an internal point-multipole molecule has no radial escaped charge.

The minimum is therefore **two prospectively fixed source families**.

## Family A: manufactured escaped-charge control

Use one spherical cavity with the production dielectric and two analytically fixed neutral radial sources:

1. **Compact internal control:** a smooth neutral spherical density with support strictly inside the cavity. Its exact result is

   [
   \gamma\phi=0,\qquad
   \beta=0,\qquad
   \sigma=0,\qquad
   G=0.
   ]

2. **Actual Gaussian mixture:** the repository Gaussian primitive, evaluated once at the center and once at one preregistered off-center position that does not alter cavity topology.

Evaluate with:

* an existing converged volumetric Poisson or exact SVPE implementation;
* the proposed escaped-charge surface implementation, if one exists;
* unchanged standard ddPCM supplied only with the boundary potential.

The centered calculation tests energy and radial volume terms. The off-center calculation tests surface coupling and the coordinate derivative.

## Family B: one fixed asymmetric molecular density

Use the already prospectively source-selected asymmetric molecule, not a molecule chosen after examining these decompositions.

From one evaluation-only QM density and the already fixed MBIS partition, construct:

[
\rho_0=\rho_{q/p}^{\rm point},
]

[
a_Q=\rho_Q^{\rm point},\qquad
a_O=\rho_O^{\rm point},
]

[
p_{\rm free}=\sum_A\delta\rho_A^{\rm free},
]

and, if the partitioned radial density is available,

[
p_{\rm QM}
==========

## \rho_{\rm QM}^{l\le1,\rm continuous}

\rho_{q/p}^{\rm point}.
]

Here:

* (a_Q,a_O) isolate local angular multipoles;
* (p_{\rm free}) is the proposed parameter-free penetration model;
* (p_{\rm QM}) is the evaluation-only true radial (l\le1) correction under the fixed QM partition.

Use the same geometry, cavity, dielectric, and numerical discretization for every source.

### Order-independent source decomposition

Let

[
B_P(x,y)=\langle x,W_Py\rangle
]

be the symmetric bilinear form from the converged Poisson/SVPE reference. Because

[
G_P[\rho]=\frac12B_P(\rho,\rho),
]

the mechanisms should not be assigned through an arbitrary sequential addition. Report instead:

[
D_Q
===

B_P(\rho_0,a_Q)
+
\frac12B_P(a_Q,a_Q),
]

[
D_O
===

B_P(\rho_0,a_O)
+
\frac12B_P(a_O,a_O),
]

[
D_{\rm pen}
===========

B_P(\rho_0,p_{\rm free})
+
\frac12B_P(p_{\rm free},p_{\rm free}),
]

together with the explicit interactions

[
D_{QO}=B_P(a_Q,a_O),
]

[
D_{Q,\rm pen}=B_P(a_Q,p_{\rm free}),
\qquad
D_{O,\rm pen}=B_P(a_O,p_{\rm free}).
]

This gives an exact, order-independent energy decomposition. The boundary MEP itself is linear, so its (Q), (O), and penetration increments can be reported directly without cross-term ambiguity.

Comparing (p_{\rm free}) with (p_{\rm QM}) on the cavity boundary and in (B_P) distinguishes:

* whether radial penetration is genuinely important;
* whether the free-atom primitive represents that radial effect;
* whether the remaining error is angular rather than radial.

### Operator and ledger decomposition

For one continuous outlying source (\rho), let:

* (\sigma_P) be the exact Poisson/SVPE surface state;
* (\sigma_D) be the state produced by standard boundary-only ddPCM.

Define the exact physical receiver evaluated at any trial surface state:

[
E_{\rm phys}(\rho,\sigma)
=========================

\frac12\langle\phi^\rho,\sigma\rangle_\Gamma
+
\frac12\langle\phi^\rho,\beta_R\rangle_{\Omega^c}.
]

Then define

[
\boxed{
D_{\rm operator}
================

## E_{\rm phys}(\rho,\sigma_D)

E_{\rm phys}(\rho,\sigma_P)
}
]

and

[
\boxed{
D_{\rm ledger}
==============

## G_D^{\rm reported}(\rho)

E_{\rm phys}(\rho,\sigma_D).
}
]

They telescope exactly:

[
\boxed{
G_D^{\rm reported}(\rho)-G_P(\rho)
==================================

D_{\rm ledger}+D_{\rm operator}.
}
]

Thus:

* (D_{\rm operator}) measures the wrong source-to-surface state;
* (D_{\rm ledger}) measures the wrong energy receiver at the state actually computed.

Neither can be hidden inside “penetration error.”

### Mandatory identities

The experiment should certify identities, not accuracy thresholds selected from the answer:

1. **PDE and interface conditions**

   [
   \nabla\cdot(\epsilon\nabla\phi)+4\pi\rho=0,
   \qquad
   [\phi]*\Gamma=0,
   \qquad
   [\epsilon\partial_n\phi]*\Gamma=0.
   ]

2. **Energy-route equality**

   [
   G
   =

   # \frac12\langle\rho,\phi_{\rm rxn}\rangle

   \frac12\langle\phi^\rho,\sigma\rangle_\Gamma
   +
   \frac12\langle\phi^\rho,\beta\rangle_{\Omega^c}.
   ]

3. **Reciprocity**

   [
   \langle\rho_1,W\rho_2\rangle
   ============================

   \langle\rho_2,W\rho_1\rangle .
   ]

4. **Charging law**

   [
   G[\lambda\rho]=\lambda^2G[\rho],
   \qquad
   \frac{dG[\lambda\rho]}{d\lambda}
   ================================

   \langle\rho,\phi_{\rm rxn}[\lambda\rho]\rangle .
   ]

5. **Coordinate derivative**

   The analytic state-adjoint derivative must converge to a central finite difference on a fixed cavity-topology branch.

6. **Compact-source zero**

   The compact neutral spherical control must converge to zero surface state and zero energy.

7. **Internal-source reduction**

   For sources strictly inside, exact SVPE must reduce to the ordinary no-escaped-charge continuum result.

Numerical decisions should be based on a preregistered refinement sequence and a posteriori discretization uncertainty. A mechanistic difference is resolved only when it remains separated from zero under refinement; otherwise the result is numerically unresolved and fails closed.

No experimental solvation value is needed anywhere in this experiment.

# III. Continuation and finite stopping

## 6. Nearest existing alternatives

| Alternative                                 | Sharp-cavity escaped charge                                       | Same declared continuum model?                               | Status for this route                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Exact SVPE / outlying-charge Poisson**    | Explicit (\beta), modified surface equation, direct volume energy | Yes                                                          | Correct terminal reference and potentially a production backend if an existing implementation passes all bindings |

The literature distinction is precise: SVPE is the exact escaped-charge surface/volume construction, whereas SS(V)PE and IEFPCM are equivalent at the integral-operator level and simulate volume polarization approximately; they become exact in the zero-escaped-charge limit. ([arXiv][1])

### Minimal defensible continuation

There are two different meanings of “continuation”:

1. **To test the penetration hypothesis:**
   Use an existing exact SVPE or volumetric Poisson implementation as an evaluation-only oracle. This preserves the same sharp cavity and dielectric and introduces no solvation parameter. Do not insert the primitive into production standard ddPCM before this test.

2. **To repair a source failure attributed to local (l\ge2):**
   Use a separately named small electrostatic head trained on independent QM density, ESP, or multipoles. Do not change the dielectric operator to compensate for a source angular deficiency.

Given the supplied evidence—quadrupoles remove a substantial part of the cavity-MEP discrepancy and octupoles remain necessary, whereas uniform dipole closure changes the source result only weakly—the penetration primitive is a legitimate final hypothesis to test, but it is not a theoretically complete substitute for missing anisotropic (l\ge2) density.

### Evidence that must bind an escaped-charge backend

An escaped-charge PCM continuation is defensible only if all of the following are fixed before chemical inspection:

1. The analytic three-dimensional source (\rho), including Gaussian normalization and nuclear terms.
2. The sharp cavity and dielectric exactly as in production.
3. The parent Poisson/SVPE scalar.
4. The full source map including (\beta_R).
5. The surface-plus-volume energy receiver.
6. State and adjoint equations.
7. All source, boundary-motion, and volume-quadrature derivatives.
8. Agreement with the manufactured Poisson reference under refinement.
9. Reciprocity, charging, ledger, and derivative identities.
10. Prospective evaluation on the already fixed source panel using its already frozen source/tail gates.

A result that improves only when (\delta V|_\Gamma) is injected into standard ddPCM but fails the exact ledger is not evidence for physical penetration. It is evidence for an operator/ledger artifact.

### Requirements for the next trained model

The trained model must replace the unsupported source category rather than act as a residual correction to solvation energies.

A suitable scalar-first construction is an invariant external-potential interaction scalar

[
\mathcal E_\theta
\left(
R,
{u_{A,lm}}_{l\le3}
\right),
]

with physical multipoles defined by derivatives

[
M_{A,lm}
========

-\frac{\partial\mathcal E_\theta}
{\partial u_{A,lm}}\bigg|_{u=0}.
]

This gives:

* SO(3)-equivariant multipoles from an invariant scalar;
* an explicit conjugate field coordinate for each (l);
* symmetric mixed response derivatives;
* a clean distinction between permanent (q/p/Q/O) and response;
* no use of latent hidden tensors as multipoles.

Training and model selection should use only molecule-disjoint QM density, ESP, distributed multipoles, or prescribed external-field electrostatic responses. Charge and molecular dipole constraints can be enforced exactly. The head must be frozen before any solvation benchmark is examined.

## 7. Finite stop rule

The zero-training route should contain exactly two remaining objects:

[
\mathcal Z
==========

\left{
\text{current POLAR-zero/MDP-}\alpha\text{-ADT candidate},
;
\text{one exact escaped-charge penetration test}
\right}.
]

There is no admissible third zero-training (l\ge2) decoder because the non-identifiability proof excludes a unique one.

Apply the following finite rule.

### Step 1: finish the current screen unchanged

Finish all twelve preregistered mechanism cases. Do not use penetration to repair a failed root, passivity, Jacobian, derivative, charge, branch, or replay test.

A mechanism failure terminates the current candidate on its own terms.

### Step 2: trigger the penetration test only under the stated condition

If the current candidate passes every mechanism gate but fails the already frozen source or chemical accuracy criterion, run the two-family exact-Poisson/SVPE experiment above.

The manufactured test must pass before any molecular result is interpreted.

### Step 3: permit exactly one panel-level penetration evaluation

Only if the exact continuum identities pass may the source

[
\rho_{q/p}^{\rm point}
+
\sum_A\delta\rho_A^{\rm free}
]

be evaluated once on the already fixed prospective 60-molecule source panel, with:

* the same cavities;
* the same dielectric equation;
* the exact escaped-charge receiver;
* the already frozen MAE/tail criteria;
* no altered thresholds.

### Step 4: terminal exhaustion conditions

Declare the zero-training near-field route exhausted if **any** of the following occurs:

1. The escaped-charge implementation fails the Poisson, reciprocity, charging, energy-ledger, or derivative identities.

2. Exact escaped-charge penetration passes the structural tests but fails the already frozen source/tail gate.

3. It passes numerically but leaves the failed cases dominated by the independently measured (Q/O) source component.

4. The free-atom radial correction does not reproduce the evaluation-only QM radial (l\le1) MEP contribution within the preregistered numerical/source criterion.

5. Apparent improvement exists only under boundary-only standard ddPCM and disappears or changes attribution under the exact surface-plus-volume ledger.

6. Further continuation would require selecting local frames, radial amplitudes, element coefficients, source scales, finite-displacement magnitudes, hidden latent tensors, clipping rules, or cavity changes not fixed by the two official checkpoints and independent atomic-density provenance.

At that point, another zero-training decoder would not be an additional physical deduction. It would be an unacknowledged model choice.

### Acceptance rule for the small trained head

Accept the separately named scalar-first (l\le3) electrostatic head as the next scientific model only when:

1. The current candidate has passed mechanism gates.
2. Operator and ledger errors have been numerically excluded or separately corrected by a bound existing method.
3. The frozen source/chemical criterion fails.
4. Exact penetration does not close that failure.
5. The independent QM (q/p/Q/O) ladder does close it, or prospectively crosses the already frozen source gate where the (q/p) source does not.

If the (q/p/Q/O) oracle also fails, then a small (l\le3) multipole head is not justified as the solution; the failure instead implicates continuous-density structure, the continuum model, or another ledger category.

If the source and matched-QM continuum quantities pass but total chemical accuracy fails, do not train an electrostatic head merely because the final observable is poor. That would be a residual correction under another name. The failed physical category must first be identified independently.

## Final adjudication

### Structural proof

* A parameter-free, stationary penetration model exists for the prescribed analytic density.
* For a sharp dielectric boundary it necessarily contains exterior volume polarization, a modified surface equation, a direct volume energy term, and their complete derivatives.
* Unchanged boundary-only ddPCM/CPCM cannot represent this same functional for general outlying density.
* No unique zero-training local (l\ge2) source is identifiable from the frozen outputs.
* ADT fixes only the (l=1), rank-at-most-three uniform-response tangent.

* Use one manufactured escaped-charge family and one fixed asymmetric molecular density.
* Resolve angular, radial, operator, and ledger contributions through exact bilinear and telescoping decompositions.
* Bind the result through Poisson, reciprocity, charging, ledger, rotational, and derivative identities under refinement.

* The penetration primitive may be tested once on the frozen source panel under the exact escaped-charge functional.
* It must not be inserted into production standard ddPCM on boundary data alone.
